## Creative direction

Aim for a 75–95 second landscape film. The phone-mounted first-person view is the main footage: both hands, the Xbox controller, and the destination screens establish that this is real input. Use matching screen recordings when viewers need to read an attachment or prompt. Do not replace the main proof with the illustrated product film.

Core message: **Two Macs. One Xbox controller. Show the AI what you mean, say what you want, and send it.**

This is a suggested shoot, not a claim that every current mapping or cross-Mac combination has been verified. Rehearse the installed version and active profile before recording. Timings are editorial targets, not promised AI response times.

## Prepare this desk

- Keep the pink controller, laptops, monitor arms, and genuine workstation setting.
- Move the cleaner bottle and loose items out of the gap between the laptops. Gather the central cable bundle behind the stands, keeping the controller cable loose enough to use comfortably.
- Move the large foreground microphone just outside the camera frame, while keeping it close enough to record clear speech.
- Replace the personal chats, work repositories, Slack, and overlapping terminals visible in the reference photo with a purpose-built, non-sensitive demo project. Hide sidebar history, account menus, and notifications; do not rely on text being too small to read.
- Bring the laptop fronts into a roughly even line if comfortable. Do not rearrange macOS display topology just for a prettier picture without rehearsing the edge actions again.
- Make the cursor larger and easy to distinguish. Enlarge the few words viewers must read. Keep the on-screen scenes simple, with an obvious visual target and generous empty space.

## Screen staging

- **Left laptop / Mac A:** Codex open to an isolated demo project, with its composer accessible.
- **Upper-left display / also Mac A:** a local preview with one large, obvious improvement to request, such as a cramped primary button. This is a second display, not a third computer.
- **Right laptop / Mac B:** a separate Codex task and a safe demo window ready for an Appshot. Pre-open both so switching does not expose private windows.
- **Upper-right display:** quiet demo content, or the Vibe Controller live map if it belongs to the lead Mac and the map is actually receiving input. Do not relocate the app across Macs for the shot.

If fitting the upper displays makes everything tiny, frame mainly the two laptops and hands. Capture the upper-display jump as a separate short insert. Do not force all four screens to be legible simultaneously.

## Appshots and dictation checks

- Appshots capture the frontmost app window and available text, not an arbitrary drag-selected rectangle. Their hotkey is both Command keys by default, or the configured custom chord. Use the actual controller binding shown in the active profile.
- The official documentation says an Appshot normally starts a new chat, but goes to a recently interacted-with chat when applicable. Activate the intended chat immediately before bringing the target window forward and taking the shot; verify the actual result during rehearsal.
- Appshot permissions must already be approved on each Mac where you demonstrate capture. Keep its target window entirely non-sensitive, including text it may expose outside the visible scroll area.
- RT invokes the target Mac’s configured dictation shortcut; the controller is not the microphone. Test the microphone and shortcut on each Mac separately. The mic used for filming is not automatically routed to the other Mac.
- Never narrate the demo while dictation is listening, except for the words you want in the prompt. Finish dictation, verify the transcription, then narrate again.

Official reference: [Appshots](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/appshots).

## Camera and sound

- Record a landscape master because the two laptops sit side by side. A separate close-framed vertical take is better than assuming a wide setup will crop cleanly for a phone feed.
- Start with the rear camera at 1×, 4K/30 fps in standard Video mode if the phone supports it. Use this as a test setting, not a guarantee against monitor flicker. Move the framing back before resorting to the ultrawide lens.
- Secure the neck mount against your chest; avoid a freely swinging phone. Sit still and hold the controller in the lower part of the frame without covering the laptop screens. Check the actual recorded framing, not just the camera preview.
- Record ten seconds of actual stick movement, a jump, and a bright app window. Play it back with sound. Check that the receiving cursor is visible, screen whites retain detail, text is readable, and there is no distracting banding or mount/clothing noise. Adjust brightness, camera exposure, frame rate, or angle and retest as needed.
- Keep focus/exposure stable once the framing works. On supported iPhones, Lock Camera prevents automatic camera switching and Lock White Balance prevents color changes. For a straightforward SDR editing workflow, turn HDR Video off. [Apple video settings](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/iphone/iphc1827d32f/26/ios/26).
- Use the existing mic for a clean narration track if practical. Clap once before the take to align phone audio, narration, and screen recordings. Test that recording software and dictation can use the chosen inputs together.
- Record the relevant displays on both Macs if feasible and permitted, using only safe demo content. These recordings are readability inserts, not substitutes for real cross-Mac proof. If recording introduces input lag, reduce recording load and rehearse again.

## Rehearsal and coverage

1. Run the full sequence once without filming. Confirm that both Macs are awake, native handoff is ready, permissions are approved, and the intended profile is active. Verify left/right/up/down destinations rather than assuming the diagram matches the physical layout.
2. Specifically test continued cursor movement and clicking after arrival on Mac B. Merely reaching its edge is not enough.
3. Complete dictation and Appshot tests on both Macs. Confirm the correct input source, chat destination, and visible attachment before the main take.
4. Reset the isolated demo project and pre-open its preview. Use a small, safe change that has already worked in rehearsal. Do not perform the demo in an employer/customer project.
5. Record one complete take, then a second. Leave roughly two seconds of stillness before and after each major action to make clean edits possible.
6. Capture close views of the capture rectangle, pasted attachment, dictated prompt, and result if the wide take is hard to read. Use the same actual take's screen recordings where possible.
7. Review the export on a phone. A desktop preview can disguise unreadable text and an invisible pointer.

Do not feel obliged to show every mapping. Copy, paste, OCR with TextSniper, Backspace, and modifier configuration can become follow-up clips. If OCR is shown, disclose that TextSniper or equivalent OCR software is a separate companion.

## Editing and launch notes

- Keep the opening cross-Mac action and the core screenshot/dictation/send chain in real time. Cut dead preparation or clearly mark compressed AI waiting time.
- Use minimal overlays: the current button/action, destination, and final URL. Do not animate a replacement cursor or simulate an attachment.
- Prefer personal, demonstrable language: “I did this without touching the keyboard” or “for this workflow.” Avoid “replaces every keyboard,” “works on any computer,” “zero setup,” and “instant” for results that take time.
- Keep any soundtrack well below speech and live dictation. Real clicks and an understandable voice are more important than a polished musical transition.
- Put the practical caveats in the post or pinned reply: public beta; current packaged lead-Mac requirements; one-time permission/virtual hardware setup on the lead Mac; Apple Universal Control requirements for cross-Mac use; no Vibe Controller install on the receiving Mac for native handoff.
- First call to action: try the GitHub beta and report controller, connection type, macOS version, and what worked or failed. Do not ask the audience to learn the entire mapping table before trying it.

Suggested post opening:

> I built a way to work in Codex with an Xbox controller. Screenshots, dictation, and jumping between my Macs—without touching the keyboard for this workflow. Vibe Controller is a public beta on GitHub. Here’s a real demo.
